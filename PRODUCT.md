## Design Context

### Users
Residents of a premium residential complex: owners and tenants who need to arrange access for guests, couriers, taxis, service workers, and cars. They usually use the app from a phone while moving through daily routines: at home, in the elevator, in a car, before a guest arrives, or when someone is already at the checkpoint.

### Brand Personality
Private, calm, precise. The interface should feel like a personal concierge service: discreet, confident, fast, and worthy of a high-end residential property. It should not feel like a CRM, security console, municipal portal, or generic SaaS dashboard.

### Design Principles
- The resident experience is mobile-first and action-first.
- The primary job is creating a pass in under a minute with minimal thought.
- Use service language: "pass", "guest", "ready", "security has it", not internal workflow jargon.
- Keep premium styling restrained: warm light surfaces, careful spacing, rare gold accents, strong readability.
- Reserve operational density, dark dashboards, and system-level data for staff roles.
- Every successful pass creation should end with a clear, reassuring confirmation and sharing action.

### Key Resident Flows
- Create a pass for a guest, courier, taxi, master, or car.
- Repeat a recent or frequent pass.
- Check active and expected passes.
- Share pass details with a guest.
- Cancel or update a pass when plans change.

### Anti-Goals
- Do not make residents interpret internal security statuses.
- Do not make the first screen a dashboard of metrics.
- Do not bury the main action inside navigation.
- Do not rely on heavy dark luxury styling inside everyday resident workflows.
