01

Custom Wordmark

Pure typography, no symbol. The hairline gap between "m" and "h" acts as a soft word-break that keeps the compound "domhub" legible without a hyphen. Draws from the Stripe / Intercom school: the brand IS the letterform. Requires the most trust in typographic craft to carry alone.

02

Wordmark + Monogram Mark Recommended

The most deployable system: a square mark (favicon, app icon, badge) paired with the wordmark for headers and print. The "dh" ligature in the square is a genuine visual idea — the bowl of "d" and the arch of "h" share a common top curve, making one form that resolves as two letters. This is the lock-up Linear, Vercel, and Stripe all converge on for good reason.

03

Arch Symbol Recommended

The arch is a pure architectural gesture — not a house, not a doorbell icon. It communicates passage, threshold, and access without literalism. The inner keystone arc in gold is deliberately restrained: one accent beat inside an otherwise monochrome form. At 32px the arch collapses gracefully to two piers + arc. The concept has genuine depth and would age well.

04

DH Monogram (Latin)

The D and H share a center vertical stroke drawn in gold — a structural ligature rather than a decorative flourish. The gold crossbar connects them as one form. Works well for international contexts where "D" and "H" are immediately readable. The weakness: at 32px the shared stroke is ambiguous and could read as "IH" or "DI".

05

Cyrillic Д Monogram

The Cyrillic Д re-read as classical column-and-lintel architecture: the base "legs" become piers with capitals, and the top strokes form a pediment. The gold apex accent marks the keystone. Strong on Russian-market materials and signals local authenticity without being provincial. Limitation: meaningless to a non-Cyrillic audience, which could constrain future growth.

06

Geometric Key Symbol

Circle (identity/ownership) + shaft (connection) + three-tier notch (access hierarchy). Reads as a key without borrowing any literal key silhouette. The three notches deliberately echo DomHub's three-tier access model (resident / staff / admin). The gold inner circle is the one decorative point — the origin of permission. Design Assessment

Honest Recommendation

02 — Wordmark + Monogram Mark  Primary pick

This is the production-ready choice. The square dh-ligature mark solves the hardest real-world problem immediately: you need a favicon, an app-icon, a badge in the nav sidebar, and a push notification icon — a wordmark alone cannot do that at 32px. The dh ligature has a genuine typographic idea behind it (shared top curve), not just two letters jammed together. The system (mark + wordmark) gives you the most deployment flexibility: use the mark alone in constrained spaces, the wordmark alone in narrow banners, and the full lock-up in headers and marketing. This is the path Vercel, Linear, and Stripe all took for identical reasons.

03 — Arch Symbol  Secondary pick / long-term direction

The strongest concept on pure brand coherence grounds. The arch is an original geometric idea that connects directly to the product's context (Замоскворечье residential architecture, gateway access, passage) without being a literal illustration. It would be the right choice if DomHub invests in building brand recognition over 3–5 years — the mark is distinctive and defensible. The risk: as a standalone symbol it requires time and marketing spend to become legible. At seed stage, the wordmark+mark of variant 02 is the safer operational bet. Revisit the arch as a brand evolution once the product is established.

Variants that should not advance

01 (Wordmark only) — The typographic concept is correct but a standalone wordmark without any mark leaves the product without a favicon-size identity. This isn't a weak concept, it's an incomplete system. Use the letterform as the basis for variant 02 instead.

04 (DH Monogram) — The shared gold stroke is a nice idea but the form reads ambiguously at small sizes — IH, DI, or an equals sign. The concept needs a stronger letter-fit or size range to work. Do not use as-is.

05 (Cyrillic Д) — The architectural reading of Д is genuinely interesting and would be a strong supplementary asset (letterhead, Russian-market materials, social media). But it should not be the primary mark because it creates a bifurcated identity problem the moment the product onboards any non-Russian operator or investor.

06 (Key) — The abstracted key concept is solid in theory (circle + shaft + three notches = ownership + connection + access hierarchy). In execution the horizontal layout creates awkward white space and the form is too similar to dozens of security/SaaS key icons. The concept is defensible but the execution needs one more round of formal development to become distinctive.

What to do next

  • Approve the direction (02 or 03) before any typography work — font choice changes everything about how the wordmark feels.
  • Commission a custom variable typeface or license one at weight 420–460 (Geist, Roobert, or ABC Whyte are strong candidates for the "Linear/Stripe" register). The SVG wordmark here uses pure-geometry strokes as a structural placeholder, not a finished typeface.
  • If going with 03 (Arch), develop the standalone arch mark in Figma at 5 sizes (16px favicon, 32px, 64px, 128px, 512px) with explicit stroke-width rules for each — the 2px stroke at 32px becomes 1px at 16px. This is non-trivial and needs a designer's eye, not just SVG math.
  • These SVGs are exploration-grade prototypes — sufficient for stakeholder alignment and Figma import, but the wordmark letterforms need a real type designer pass before production use.
  • The color system (warm dark, single gold accent) is already excellent and should not change. Do not let a printer or vendor talk you into CMYK gold conversions — specify Pantone 871 C (metallic) for print use.
